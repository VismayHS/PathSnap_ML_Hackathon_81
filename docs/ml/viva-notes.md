# Scientific explanation for the team

The application converts noisy GPS observations into directed road positions. Each trip has its own network: arc12 in one trip is unrelated to arc12 in another. Preserve IDs and observation indices throughout.

1. **What is ground truth?** The supplied reference is an ordered road traversal, not a label array aligned to GPS rows. Our offline monotone alignment derives provisional observation targets, preserving repeated road occurrences. We expose scorable and unscorable counts because organizer scoring is not yet confirmed.
2. **What can inference read?** Only track, nodes and arcs. Candidate generation, features, the fitted classifier and Viterbi never load references. After prediction, the separate scorer reads the reference and returns aggregate metrics. Development Folium exports are separate and contain references; they must never be served as evaluator/UI inputs.
3. **Why a projection?** Latitude/longitude degrees are not metres. Each trip uses a local WGS84 azimuthal-equidistant projection with explicit longitude/latitude order. Geometry is straight between supplied nodes; unavailable bends or traffic rules are not invented.
4. **How are candidates found?** Length-binned cKDTree segment-midpoint queries conservatively over-select, followed by exact point-to-segment distance refinement. A100m radius and top8 cap apply consistently; ties use original arc IDs. Zero-length arcs stay in topology but cannot be geometric candidates.
5. **What is learned?** A binary candidate-relevance estimator uses distance, heading, length, projection fraction, speed, time gap and distance relative to the nearest candidate. Arc IDs and reference targets never become features. Relevance is normalized within each observation and is not calibrated confidence.
6. **Why whole-trip splits?** Adjacent GPS rows are strongly related. Five fixed training-trip group folds prevent the same trip appearing in fit and validation. Imputation/scaling is fitted within each fold. Only fit observations are capped (400 per trip); validation/student testing are unsampled.
7. **Why these five models?** LR tests a regularized linear boundary, KNN local feature neighborhoods, RF bagged trees, Extra Trees randomized-threshold trees, and HGB boosted histogram trees. These are five distinct algorithms. The HMM is a decoder, not a sixth fitted classifier. See model-selection.md for measured results.
8. **Why HGB?** It led the predefined training-CV trip Macro F1 at modest measured cost. Its lead over RF was small. The decision was not based on student-test accuracy, where RF exceeded untuned HGB.
9. **What makes tuning Bayesian?** Optuna TPE ran four startup trials, then eight adaptive trials informed by previous trial outcomes, each evaluated on all five fixed training folds. All12 outcomes and the SQLite study are retained. An interrupted last trial was recovered using its already sampled parameters; it was not resampled or silently dropped.
10. **How does the decoder work?** States are candidate directed arcs/projected positions. Log emissions come from the fitted relevance model. Transition scores penalize disagreement between directed graph travel distance and GPS displacement. Dijkstra includes partial arcs; Viterbi finds the best sequence. Invalid rows, no candidates, large time gaps and unreachable transitions break/restart blocks. Output geometry follows real graph arcs and keeps gaps separate.
11. **What do the numbers mean?** Accuracy pools scorable observations including misses; Macro F1 averages trip-local road-class macro values. Conditional log loss excludes absent-reference candidates, whose miss rate is separate. Sequence similarity compares compressed observed-arc runs, not the entire traveled route. Continuity checks the supplied directed graph and generous fixed travel bound, not real-world turn restrictions.
12. **What actually improved?** Under provisional development scoring, tuned classifier versus HMM accuracy was86.04% versus88.55%; mean trip Macro F1 was0.7706 versus0.8511; observed-arc sequence similarity was0.6387 versus0.8559. HMM log loss stays identical because it is the same classifier-emission metric, not a posterior metric. These are not reserved evaluator claims.
13. **What remains outside local verification?** Official pointwise scoring clarification, reserved evaluator data and the two-physical-laptop demonstration. UI/API/Folium/PDF integration has its own explicit review records; scientific unit tests do not establish physical LAN success.
